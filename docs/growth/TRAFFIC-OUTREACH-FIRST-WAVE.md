## Shared Principles

- Keep pitches short and specific.
- Do not mention backlinks in the opening email.
- Do not pitch homepage links.
- Offer one article idea per site, not multiple vague options.
- Position RoleSharp as a useful resource, not as the point of the article.

## Article Outline 1

### Title

How to make your resume ATS-friendly without making it generic

### Best-fit sites

- CareerAddict
- hipCV
- ResumeGo

### Best RoleSharp target

- `/resume-templates`
- secondary option: `/blog/ats-friendly-resume-2026`

### Safe anchor

- `ATS-safe resume templates`

### Outline

#### 1. Introduction

- why ATS advice often goes wrong
- the tradeoff people imagine between readability and personality

#### 2. What ATS-friendly actually means

- readable structure
- standard headings
- no layout tricks that break parsing
- natural keyword alignment

#### 3. Where people overcorrect

- stuffing keywords
- flattening all bullets into generic phrasing
- removing useful detail to sound "clean"

#### 4. How to keep specificity

- use real achievements
- tailor bullets to the job description
- keep role language relevant without copying the posting

#### 5. The three resume sections that matter most

- summary
- experience
- skills

#### 6. A final ATS-safe checklist

- formatting
- wording
- keyword coverage
- proofread pass

#### 7. Conclusion

- ATS-safe should mean clearer and stronger, not more generic
